The Model Builder

Every model in this book is built from four shapes. You stretch them, turn them, paint them and stick them together.

Cube, sphere, cylinder and tetrahedron, just as they arrive. The orange hammer above each one is its toolbox.
Cubewalls, roofs, bodies
Sphereheads, shells, domes
Cylinderwheels, legs, columns
Tetrahedronnoses, tails, points
A tetrahedron is a pyramid with a triangle at the bottom instead of a square, so it has four flat faces. Every shape starts out 2 feet across – just under half your height.
Make a shape
  1. Open Create Model ▸ in the Menu.
  2. Click a shape. It lands about 10 feet in front of you, painted yellow.
  3. Click its hammer to open the toolbox.
The hammer menu
Apply TexturePaint it a colour, or wrap a picture round it
Stretch to ShapeMake it longer, wider, taller – and lift it
Rotate/Move ShapeTurn it on its side, or round
Connect to PrimitiveJoin it to the pieces it's touching
Remove ShapeDelete it
Render ModelTurn all the joined pieces into one model
A shape that hasn't been rendered is a piece, not a model: clicking the piece itself does nothing, so students work through the hammer. After Render Model, the finished model opens the normal menu (Chapter 5).
Move 1: Stretch

Choose Stretch to Shape. A see-through blue box appears, with a handle on every corner.

Drag a corner and the piece grows that way. The opposite corner stays put, so nothing slides away.

Drag the middle of the box to slide the piece along the ground.

Press ✓ Done stretching when you've finished.

Move 2: Lift

Above the blue box floats a green ball. Drag it up to lift the piece into the air, or sideways to slide it along at that height.

That's how a head goes on a body and a roof goes on a house. A lifted piece stays at its height when you slide it.

Stretch a cube into a flat floor, then a tall wall, then a long beam. Which corner did you drag each time, and which way?
Move 3: Turn

Choose Rotate/Move Shape. Three rings appear.

The flat yellow ring spins the piece round like a top. The two standing rings tip it over.

Turns snap every 15 degrees. Six snaps make a right angle (90°), which lays a shape down on its side.

If a ring is hard to grab, it's probably edge-on to you. Walk a couple of steps sideways and try again.
Paint it

Apply Texture offers seven colours. For any other colour – black, brown, grey – click the box next to Any colour.

Upload an Image wraps a picture around the shape. You'll use it for a sign in Chapter 11.

Warm-up: a snowman

Four pieces and all three moves. It's the best first build there is.

  1. Add three spheres and a tetrahedron.
  2. Make the spheres three sizes. Stretch the first one bigger by dragging a top corner up and out. Leave the second as it is. Shrink the third by dragging a corner in.
  3. Stack them. Slide the middle sphere next to the big one, then lift it with the green ball until it sits on top, sunk in a little. Do the same with the small one.
  4. Paint the spheres white with Apply Texture.
Three spheres in three sizes, and a tiny tetrahedron for a nose.
  1. Make the nose. Shrink the tetrahedron right down, paint it orange, and push it into the front of the head.
  2. Join them. Hammer on the middle sphere, then Connect to Primitive. It lists only the pieces it's touching. Join them all.
  3. Render it. Hammer on any piece, then Render Model. The hammers vanish: it's one snowman now.
Connect to Primitive says how many pieces the model has so far. Rendered: one model.
Pieces have to touch to join. A head floating a hair above the body won't appear in the Connect list – push it down until it sinks in a little. After Render Model, the pieces can't be stretched or painted any more. Get everything right first. Build a second snowman beside the first, exactly half its size. Is its head half the size of the big one's head?

Build a train

A steam engine with six wheels, a chimney and a cab – and a carriage to pull. In Part 4 you'll program it to drive.

The finished engine and carriage. The engine is about 7 feet long: you could almost ride it. Build in My World (edusim3dweb.com/app/?world=8), an open field with nothing in the way. Allow 40–50 minutes for the engine and 15 for the carriage.
What you'll need

The engine: 12 pieces

boiler (green) wheels (yellow) cab (red) cab roof (black) chimney (black) dome (orange) cowcatcher (red)

The carriage: 6 pieces

body (blue) roof (white) wheels (yellow)
A model's front is the side facing back towards where you arrived in the world. Build the engine with its cowcatcher pointing at you, and in Part 4 it will drive forwards, not backwards. The cowcatcher is the pointed frame on the front of a steam engine. It pushed things off the track so they couldn't get under the wheels. Find every piece in this list in the picture on the last page. Which ones are partly hidden? Works well in pairs: one reads each step aloud, one drives the mouse. Swap at every step.
Step 1: The boiler

The boiler is a cylinder lying on its side, pointing at you.

  1. Add a cylinder. It arrives standing up.
  2. Lay it down. Choose Rotate/Move Shape and drag one of the standing rings six snaps (90°), until the round end points straight at you.
  3. Make it longer. Choose Stretch to Shape and pull the corner nearest you towards you, until the boiler is about three times as long as it is wide.
  4. Paint it green.
The boiler, laid on its side with the rings. The flat yellow ring would spin it; the standing rings tip it. Before you drag, point to the ring you think will lay it down. Were you right? That's turning a shape in your head.
Step 2: Six wheels

A wheel is a cylinder too – but turned the other way, so its flat face looks sideways.

  1. Add a cylinder and turn it six snaps on the other standing ring. Its flat face should point to the side, not at you.
  2. Squash it thin with Stretch to Shape, and make it a little smaller than it started.
  3. Paint it yellow and slide it against the side of the boiler, near the front, with its top hidden under the boiler.
  4. Make five more the same way: three down each side.
The boiler and six wheels. Each still has its own hammer. Make one wheel perfect first. Then make each new wheel by looking at that one: same size, same height, same distance from the boiler.
Step 3: The cab

The cab is where the driver stands, at the back of the engine.

  1. Add a cube. Stretch it taller than the boiler and a little wider.
  2. Slide it to the back of the boiler so the two overlap, and lift it with the green ball until its bottom sits level with the bottom of the boiler.
  3. Paint it red.
  4. Add the roof: another cube, squashed very flat and stretched a little wider than the cab. Lift it on top and paint it black with Any colour.
Stretching the cab. The green ball on top is how you lift it.
Step 4: The details
  1. Chimney: a cylinder, squeezed thin and a little taller. Lift it on top of the boiler near the front, sunk in, and paint it black.
  2. Dome: a sphere, squashed a little flatter. Sit it on top of the boiler in the middle, sunk in, and paint it orange.
  3. Cowcatcher: a tetrahedron. Turn it on the flat yellow ring until a point faces forward, stretch it wide, and slide it low under the front of the boiler. Paint it red.
All twelve pieces in place, still with their hammers. Walk all the way round your engine before you join anything. Is every wheel touching? Is anything floating? It's much easier to fix now.
Step 5: Join it and render it
  1. Hammer on the boiler, then Connect to Primitive. Join every piece in the list.
  2. Hammer on the cab and join its roof the same way.
  3. Check the count. The note at the bottom of the Connect list says how many pieces the model has. It should say 12. Fewer? Something isn't touching.
  4. Render Model. The hammers vanish and you have one engine.
One engine. Click it now and the normal menu opens: Bring it to life, Camera, Program. A piece missing from the list is almost always floating a tiny bit away. Open Stretch to Shape on it and slide or lift it until it sinks into its neighbour.
Step 6: The carriage

Build the carriage behind the engine, so you can match its size and height by eye.

  1. Body: a cube stretched long, about as wide as the cab. Lift it so its bottom matches the bottom of the cab. Paint it blue.
  2. Roof: a cylinder laid on its side, pointing the same way as the boiler, squashed flat and stretched as long as the body. Lift it on top and paint it white.
  3. Four wheels, made exactly like the engine's.
  4. Connect and render. The carriage is its own model, separate from the engine.
The rendered engine, with the carriage pieces still being built behind it.
Take it for a drive

Before you write any code, try driving your engine yourself.

  1. Click the engine, then Camera, then Drive it.
  2. Steer with the arrow keys: ↑ goes forward, ← and → turn.
  3. Press Esc or ✓ Done driving to stop.
Driving the engine: the camera rides along behind it. If the engine goes backwards when you press ↑, it was built facing away from you. You can still program it in Part 4 – just start every program with rotate [180] degrees.
A steam engine needs coal, and the coal rides in a tender right behind the cab. Build one with no steps – just this picture and the parts.
body (green) coal, squashed flat (black) wheels (yellow)

  • A diesel engine: one long box instead of a round boiler.

Build an animal

A turtle is a squashed ball with a head, four legs and a tail. Building one teaches the most useful trick in modelling: turning one shape into a different one.

The finished turtle: nine pieces.
What you'll need
shell (green) head (yellow) legs (yellow) tail (yellow) eyes (black)
Every new shape arrives yellow, so the head, legs and tail need no painting at all. Only the shell and the eyes do.
Picture it first

Before you start, picture a ball squashed flat like a cushion, then pulled longer from front to back. That's the shell. Everything else hangs off it.

a ball

squashed: a shell
Look at the picture of the finished turtle. How much of each leg can you see? How much is hidden inside the shell? Plan to hide the same amount. Build the turtle with its head towards you, just like the engine, so it walks forwards in Part 4.
Step 1: The shell
  1. Add a sphere and choose Stretch to Shape.
  2. Make it wider: drag a side corner outwards until it's about one and a half times as wide.
  3. Make it longer: drag a front corner towards you until it's a bit longer than it is wide.
  4. Squash it down: drag a top corner down until it's only about half as tall as it is wide.
  5. Paint it green.
A sphere, stretched wide, long and flat. It's a shell now, not a ball.
Step 2: The head
  1. Add a sphere, about a third the size of the shell, and stretch it a little longer from front to back.
  2. Slide it to the front of the shell, low down, so its back half disappears under the edge of the shell.
Step 3: Four legs
  1. Add a cylinder and shrink it into a short, stubby leg.
  2. Slide it under one corner of the shell, so the top of the leg is hidden inside.
  3. Make three more, one under each corner.
Down at an ant's height: the shell, the head and four stubby legs. Stand right in front of the turtle and crouch down to an ant's height. Do all four legs look the same length? Are they the same distance from the head?
Step 4: The tail and the eyes
  1. Tail: a tetrahedron, shrunk small and stretched a little long. Push it into the back of the shell, low down.
  2. Eyes: two tiny spheres. Shrink each right down, paint it black with Any colour, and push it into the front of the head.
All nine pieces, ready to join.
Step 5: Join and render

Hammer on the shell, then Connect to Primitive, and join everything. Then join the eyes to the head. Check the count says 9, then Render Model.

Bring your turtle to life

Your turtle is a model now, just like the geese in the Park. Click it and choose Bring it to life:

  • Breathe makes it swell and shrink gently, as if it's breathing.
  • Watch me makes it turn to follow you as you walk round it.
  • Hold still stops it.
Real sea turtles can't pull their heads into their shells the way some land turtles can. They swim with their front flippers and steer with their back ones.
No steps this time: here's a giraffe and its parts. The hard part is the neck, which leans forward. Use one of the standing rings to tip it two snaps (30°).
body (orange) long legs (yellow) neck, tipped forward head (orange) horns (brown) tail (brown)
Any animal is a set of shapes

Look at a real animal and ask: which of my four shapes is each part closest to?

PenguinA tall egg-shaped sphere body, a smaller sphere head, a tetrahedron beak, two flat cube flippers.
SnailA sphere shell, a long squashed cylinder body, two thin cylinder feelers with tiny spheres on top.
DogA long cube body, four cylinder legs, a cube head with a smaller cube nose, two flat ears.
FishA sphere stretched long, a tetrahedron tail, two small tetrahedron fins.
BirdA sphere body, a smaller sphere head, a tetrahedron beak, two flattened sphere wings.
CaterpillarSix spheres in a row, each sunk into the next, with two tiny feelers.

Animal builds are a natural partner for science: ask students to build an animal from a habitat they're studying, then present which shape they chose for each body part and why.

Build a building

Your train needs somewhere to stop. A station is a platform, four columns and a roof – and a sign with a real picture on it.

The station, with the engine and carriage pulled up beside it.
What you'll need
platform (grey) columns (white) roof (red) sign (a picture)
You are the ruler

You're 5 feet tall in Edusim. A station roof needs to be higher than your head, so stand next to your columns while you stretch them and make them taller than you.

Standing on the finished platform: the roof is well above your head. A real station platform is built at the same height as the train's floor, so people can step straight on without climbing.
Step 1: The platform
  1. Add a cube and stretch it long – about five times as long as it started – and twice as deep.
  2. Squash it flat, to under half its height.
  3. Paint it grey with Any colour.
Step 2: Four columns
  1. Add a cylinder, squeeze it thin, and stretch it tall – taller than you.
  2. Lift it so it stands on the platform, near one corner. Paint it white.
  3. Make three more, one for each corner, all the same height.
The platform and four columns, before the roof.
Step 3: The roof
  1. Add a cube and squash it very flat.
  2. Stretch it a little longer and wider than the platform, so it hangs over the edges.
  3. Lift it with the green ball until it rests on top of all four columns. Paint it red.
Lifting the roof onto the columns. Walk under your roof. If it touches the top of your head, lift it higher – and stretch the columns up to meet it. If the roof keeps sinking into one column and floating above another, the columns aren't the same height. Fix the columns first, then lift the roof again.
Step 4: A sign with a picture on it

First you need a picture of your sign, saved on your computer. Two ways to make one:

  • Draw it on a computer in any paint program, and save it as a picture.
  • Write it on paper in big letters, photograph it, and save the photo.
  1. Add a cube, stretch it wide, squash it very thin from front to back, and lift it onto the middle of the roof.
  2. Hammer, then Apply Texture, then Upload an Image, and choose your picture.
The sign wearing its picture, with Apply Texture still open. A picture wraps around every side of the shape. That's why the sign is squashed thin: the picture you see is on its big front and back faces.
Step 5: Join and render

Join everything to the platform, check the count says 7, and render. Then drive your engine up beside the platform with Camera and Drive it.

All aboard! The engine, the carriage and the station, together. You've now built an engine, a carriage, a turtle and a station: 34 pieces in all, from just four shapes and three moves. Take a photo of your train standing at the platform, and hang it on the station wall (Chapter 3).
Every station needs a town. Build a house with a pointed roof, a door and windows. The roof needs one clever trick – this page shows you how.
The roof trick

There's no roof-shaped shape. But a cube turned 45° on its side has a sharp edge on top, and if you sink its bottom half inside the walls, what's left looks exactly like a pointed roof.

  1. Make the walls: a cube stretched into a box a little taller than you.
  2. Make the roof: another cube, stretched as long as the walls are wide.
  3. Turn it 45° (three snaps) on the standing ring that tips it towards you.
  4. Stretch it until its sides reach out past the walls, then lift it until its middle sits on top of the walls.
The roof cube, turned three snaps, sitting on the walls.
The finished house: walls, the roof trick, a door, four windows and a chimney.
walls (yellow) roof, turned 45° (red) door (brown) windows (blue) chimney (white)
Your walls are a solid block. A window pushed too far in disappears inside it and can never be seen. Squash each window very thin and push it in only just far enough to touch the wall. Walk through your door. You can't – it's painted on, and the house is solid inside. What would you need to build to make a house you can walk into?

Skills check

Can you do these without looking back?

  • Make each of the four shapes.
  • Stretch a cube into a wall.
  • Lay a cylinder on its side, pointing at you.
  • Lift one shape on top of another.
  • Paint a shape black using Any colour.
  • Join pieces, check the count, and render a model.

Build something from your classroom – a chair, a desk, a globe, a pencil – without saying what it is. Can a friend guess it by walking round it?
