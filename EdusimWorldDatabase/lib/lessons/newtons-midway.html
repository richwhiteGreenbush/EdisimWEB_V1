🎒 Student lesson guide

Getting there: press Open this world in Edusim on this page. No account or download. Type it in: edusim3dweb.com/worlds/world.php?slug=newton-s-midway Opening a world replaces what is in your copy. Use ☰ Menu → Save World first if you have something to keep.

How it works: walk with the arrow keys and drag to look around. Click a ride and press Program to see its blocks. The green blocks at the top are its numbers. Change one, press Save, and it restarts with your number. Press a green ▶ to run a ride again.

Three rules of this lab: one foot of this world is one metre. There is no air resistance. Time is the program's own clock, t, so read the number the ride says, not your watch.

🍎 Teacher lesson card

ForGrades 9–12 physics, physical science or computer science. No coding experience needed: students start by changing one number in a green block, and three activities have them build a program from empty.
TimeOne 45-minute period for three stations, a 90-minute block for all six, or one station a day as a lab opener through a mechanics unit.
You needA browser on each computer (Chromebooks are fine). No account, install or student data. A calculator, and printed copies of the student guide or a way to type answers.
Students willPredict and measure free fall and projectile range. Use F = ma with friction. Track energy between height, motion and heat. Show momentum is conserved in a collision when motion energy is not. Find what sets a pendulum's period and a swing ride's lean. Explain why a stepped model differs from a formula.
Say this out loud. This lab uses 1 foot of the world for 1 metre, so students are five-metre giants and distances look smaller than they feel. There is no air resistance. Time is the model's own clock: the same numbers come out on every computer, but a ride can run faster or slower than a stopwatch, so read the number the ride says. Results are step-by-step approximations, a little different from the formula on purpose. This is mechanics only, with no waves or electricity. Research supports 3D learning environments in general, but nobody has tested this particular world.

Discussion prompts

  • The range is the same at 30° and 60°. What does each shot trade away to get there?
  • Friction takes the same fraction of a cart's weight whatever it weighs, so why is the heavy cart hurt more when the thrust is fixed?
  • The stuck-together crash loses most of its motion energy and none of its momentum. Where did the energy go, and why can't momentum go there too?
  • A coarse dt gave a wrong arc with no error message. How would you know to distrust a model like that?

Answer key

Show the answers

Each "model" value is what the ride says. The formula value is in brackets where the two differ.

1 Drop TowerFrom 20 m on Earth: 2.02 s at 19.80 m/s. Formula t = √(2h/g). Mars: 3.28 s at 12.17 m/s. Moon: 4.96 s at 8.04 m/s (4.97 s, 8.05 m/s). From 10 m: 1.42 s (1.43 s). Time goes as the square root of height, so doubling the height multiplies the time by about 1.41. Mass is not in the formula at all.
2 CannonAt 20 m/s, the range for 15°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 75° is 20.09, 35.33, 40.73, 35.20, 20.39 m (20.41, 35.35, 40.82, 35.35, 20.41). Pairs that add to 90° land together, because sin 2θ is the same for both. vx has no force on it. To land at 30 m at 45°, speed = √(Rg) = 17.15 m/s, which the model lands at 29.83 m, on the ring. With dt = 0.5 the range falls to 35.36 m and the top from 10.06 m to 6.79 m, and the arc is visibly a few straight lines. A smaller dt is more accurate and takes more steps.
3 Test Track100 kg: a = 4 m/s², 4.48 s (4.47 s), 17.92 m/s. 200 kg: a = 2 m/s², 6.32 s. 400 kg: a = 1 m/s², 8.94 s. Time goes as 1/√a, so 4 times the mass doubles the time. With mu = 0.05: a = 3.51, 1.51, 0.51 m/s² and 4.78, 7.28, 12.52 s. Friction is mu × m × g, so it grows with mass while the thrust does not. At mu = 0.5 on 100 kg, friction is 490 N against 400 N of thrust, so the "if a ≤ 0" block reports that the cart cannot move.
4 CoasterHeight 15, mu 0: 17.15 m/s, back to 15.00 m. mu 0.05: 16.38 m/s, back to 12.61 m. mu 0.1: 15.59 m/s, back to 10.57 m. Height 10, mu 0: 14.00 m/s, back to 10.00 m. v = √(2gh). Doubling the mass changes nothing the pod does, because mass cancels; the total energy doubles from 73,500 J to 147,000 J but the bars show shares of it. Lost energy is heat, shown in the third bar, and the three bars always add to 100%.
5 Bumper Carsm1 = 150, m2 = 300, u1 = 4, u2 = 1. Momentum 300 kg m/s before and after in every case. e = 1: car A −2.67 m/s (it bounces back), car B 2.33 m/s, motion energy 1,350 J before and after. e = 0: both 0.67 m/s, motion energy 1,350 J down to 100 J. Momentum is conserved in both; motion energy only when e = 1. The rest becomes heat and sound.
6 Swing RidesFormula for 12 m: 6.95 s. Model: 12 m at 30° 7.08 s, at 5° 6.96 s, at 60° 7.47 s; 6 m at 30° 5.01 s (formula 4.92 s). The formula assumes small swings, so 5° agrees best. The swing ride leans 50.3° at a period of 6, 63.7° at 5, and 0° at 8: below a certain speed (a period of about 7.5 s for 14 m chains) the chain has no need to lean, so it hangs straight. The tension in the chain has a part pointing to the centre, and that is the centripetal force.

Adjusting it

  • Conceptual physics: skip the formulas. Use the predict-and-vote questions and the energy bars, and stop at "what changed and why".
  • Algebra-based physics: have students derive each formula first, and treat the ride as the check on their algebra.
  • AP-leaning: add air drag to the cannon with one more block, or graph the model's error against dt and find its order.
  • Computer science: compare the velocity-first update with position-first, and find out which one gains energy on the pendulum.
  • Handing work in: ☰ Menu → Save World downloads a file you can open and inspect, programs included.

Quick troubleshooting

  • A red name appears in a block, or a message says nothing has set a value: a variable was renamed in one place and not another. Pick the right name from the block's little arrow and press Save.
  • The cannon does nothing: it fires once per run. Press its green ▶, or Save again.
  • The sky is full of arcs: reopen the world, since marks are not saved. Each shot adds one on purpose so angles can be compared.
  • The pod leaves its track: height is above 18, or slope was changed. The track is built at 30°.
  • The ride looks slower than the guide says: a slower computer runs the same model in slow motion. The numbers do not change.
  • The world has been changed: reopen it from the gallery for a fresh copy.
