Build-Along Tutorials

Six projects that start with four snowballs and finish with a robot that copies itself. Open Edusim in one window, keep this in another, and work down the list. Nothing here needs anything you have to buy, download or sign up for.

Before you start: the five things that do everything

Every tutorial below is made of these five moves and nothing else. Read this once and you can skip straight to whichever project you like the look of.

  1. 1 Add a shape

    ☰ Menu → Create Model → Cube, Sphere, Cylinder or Tetrahedron. It lands on the grass a few steps in front of you, bright yellow, about knee high. Yellow means "still being built".

  2. 2 Click the hammer

    Every unfinished piece has an orange 🔨 floating over it. Click it and you get the Build Piece menu: Apply Texture, Stretch to Shape, Rotate/Move Shape, Connect to Primitive, Remove Shape, Render Model, Close. Remove Shape is the undo button — it takes that one piece back out of the world and leaves everything else alone.

  3. 3 Shape it and move it

    Stretch to Shape wraps the piece in a see-through blue box. That one box does three jobs:

    • A corner handle — drag to stretch. The opposite corner stays put.
    • The middle of the box — drag to slide the piece about. It keeps whatever height it is at.
    • The green ball above — drag to move the piece anywhere. Up and down to raise and lower it, sideways to slide it around in mid-air until it lines up with the piece underneath.

    Press ✓ Done stretching or Esc when the piece looks right.

    Rotate/Move Shape is the other half of the same job. It wraps the piece in three coloured rings: drag the flat amber one to spin it on the spot, and the upright red and violet ones to tip it over. Turning snaps every 15°, so a 45° roof brace lands at exactly 45°. The blue box and the green ball are still there too, so you can turn a piece and nudge it into place without switching screens.

  4. 4 Paint it

    Apply Texture — tap a colour, mix your own, or Upload an Image to wrap a picture from your computer right around the shape.

  5. 5 Join and render

    Connect to Primitive lists only the pieces this one is actually touching. Join them all up, then press Render Model on any one of them and the whole chain becomes a single solid model — one you can click, resize, move and program like anything else in the world.

⚠️ Render Model is a one-way door. Once you press it the pieces become one object and the hammers disappear, so leave it until the very end. If two pieces are floating slightly apart, push them together before you render — Connect will not list a piece that isn't touching.

Tutorial 1 · Easy · about 15 minutes

⛄ Build a Snowman

Four balls, a hat and a carrot. The best first build there is — you learn stacking, painting and joining without stretching anything at all.

  1. Add three spheres. Menu → Create Model → Sphere, three times. They fan out in front of you.
  2. Make them three different sizes. Hammer → Stretch to Shape on the second one, then drag a corner handle inwards until it is about three-quarters the size of the first. Do the same to the third, but smaller again. Press ✓ Done each time.
  3. Stack the middle ball. Hammer on the medium sphere → Stretch to Shape. Drag the middle of the blue box until it is right beside the big one, then drag the green ball upwards until it sits on top. It will not be centred — nudge the same green ball sideways until it is. Let it sink in a little too: a snowman with a gap looks wrong, and pieces have to touch to be joined.
  4. Stack the head the same way, on top of the middle ball.
  5. Paint them white. Hammer → Apply Texture → the white swatch, on all three.
  6. Add a hat. Two cylinders: squash one flat with a top corner handle for the brim, leave the other taller for the crown. Lift both onto the head, paint them black.
  7. Face and buttons. Two tiny spheres for eyes, small ones down the front for buttons — shrink them right down with a corner handle, lift them into place and paint them black. For the nose, take a cube, squash it thin and long, paint it orange.
  8. Arms. Two more cubes, squashed into long thin sticks, painted brown, one each side.
  9. Join everything. Hammer on the middle ball → Connect to Primitive. It lists everything touching it — join them. Work outwards until every piece is connected to a neighbour.
  10. Render. Hammer on any piece → Render Model. The hammers vanish and you have a snowman.

🎯 Now change something: click your finished snowman and choose Size → 300%. A snowman three times the size of a person is a very different thing to stand next to. Then try 40%.

Tutorial 2 · Easy · about 20 minutes

🚀 Build a Rocket

Eight pieces. This is the one that teaches stretching properly — a rocket is nothing but a cylinder pulled tall and some cubes squashed flat.

  1. The body. Add a Cylinder. Stretch to Shape, then drag a top corner handle straight up until it is about three times as tall as it is wide. Because the opposite corner stays still, the bottom never leaves the ground. Paint it white.
  2. The nose. Add a Sphere. Stretch it taller than it is wide so it becomes an egg, then use the green ball to lift it onto the top of the body until it overlaps a little. Paint it red.
  3. The engine. Add a Cylinder, squash it short and make it slightly wider than the body, and leave it at the bottom. Paint it dark grey.
  4. Four fins. Add a Cube. Squash it thin in one direction so it becomes a flat plate, then stretch it into a fin shape. Slide it against the bottom of the body. Repeat three more times, one on each side — front, back, left, right. Paint them all red.
  5. A porthole. A small sphere pushed halfway into the body, painted blue.
  6. Connect everything to the body, then Render Model.

💡 Stuck on a fin? Fins are fiddly because you're stretching in one direction and sliding in another. Walk around to the other side of the rocket before you drag — the corner nearest the camera is always the easiest one to grab, and the axis pointing away from you is the hardest to stretch.

🎯 Now change something: build a second, smaller rocket beside the first, then give the big one repeat 30 times → move Y by 0.4 feet and watch it launch.

Tutorial 3 · Medium · about 30 minutes

🤖 Build a Robot

Twelve pieces, and the model on the front page of this site. Take it slowly — the trick is to build from the ground up, so every new piece has something underneath it to line up against.

  1. Two legs. Two cylinders, each squashed thin and stretched to about knee height. Slide them side by side, about a foot apart. Paint them dark grey.
  2. The body. A cube stretched a bit wider than the two legs together and squashed shallow front-to-back, then lifted with the green ball until it rests on top of them. Paint it red.
  3. The head. A smaller cube lifted onto the body. Paint it white.
  4. Two eyes. Small spheres pushed into the front of the head. Paint them blue.
  5. A mouth. A cube squashed into a thin bar, pushed into the head just below the eyes. Paint it black.
  6. Two arms. Cylinders like the legs but shorter, slid against the sides of the body. Check they touch — this is the step everybody gets wrong, and a floating arm will not appear in the Connect list.
  7. A chest panel. A flat cube pressed onto the front of the body, painted yellow.
  8. An antenna. A very thin cylinder standing on the head, with a small sphere on top. Paint the sphere green.
  9. Connect everything. Legs and arms and chest to the body; eyes, mouth and antenna to the head; head to the body; the ball to the antenna. Open Connect to Primitive on the body and it should say the model is a dozen pieces.
  10. Render Model.

💡 Check your work before you render. Open Connect to Primitive on any piece. The note at the bottom tells you how many pieces are in the model so far. If it says fewer than you built, something isn't joined — find it, slide it until it touches, and connect it.

🎯 Now change something: give your robot a face of its own. Apply Texture → Upload an Image on the head, and pick a photo. Do it before you render.

Tutorial 4 · Medium · about 30 minutes

🏚️ Build a House

Almost all cubes, and the first build that is bigger than you are. Remember that you are five feet tall in this world — use yourself as the tape measure.

  1. The floor. A cube stretched wide and long and squashed almost flat. Make it about four times your own width across. Paint it grey.
  2. The back wall. A cube stretched to the same width, squashed thin front-to-back, and stretched up to about twice your height. Slide it to the back edge of the floor. Paint it a wall colour.
  3. Two side walls. The same idea turned ninety degrees — thin the other way. Slide one to each end.
  4. The front, with a doorway. Don't make one big wall. Make three: a narrow one on the left, a narrow one on the right, and a short one across the top between them. The gap you leave is the door — walk through it and check it is wide enough before you go any further.
  5. A roof. A cube stretched wider and longer than the walls, squashed flat, and lifted with the green ball until it sits on top. Paint it dark.
  6. Windows. Flat cubes pressed onto the outside of the walls, painted pale blue. Push them into the wall face so they sit proud of it, not inside it.
  7. Connect everything to the floor, then Render Model.

💡 A window has to be on the outside of a wall. Your walls are solid blocks, so a window pushed too far in disappears inside the wood and can never be seen from anywhere you can stand. Push it just far enough to overlap.

🎯 Now change something: put a light inside. Menu → Load Object → Light Orb, then click the orb and use Move to walk it in through your door. Hang it about head height — down low it lights the floor and nothing else.

Tutorial 5 · Coding · about 15 minutes

🧩 Teach it to move

You have a model. Now make it do something. This works on anything at all — your robot, a park bench, or a Tyrannosaurus.

  1. Click your model and choose Program. The block editor opens: a palette of blocks on the left, empty space on the right.
  2. Drag forever across. It has a mouth — anything you drop inside it repeats for good.
  3. Drop rotate 15 degrees inside it, then wait 0.1 seconds underneath that, still inside.
  4. Press Save. It starts turning immediately.
  5. Change the numbers. Try 2 degrees. Try 90. Try taking the wait out altogether and see how much faster it goes.
  1. forever
  2. rotate 15 degrees
  3. wait 0.1 seconds

⚠️ The one rule that catches everybody: move X, move Y and move Z slide an object along the world's directions, not the way it happens to be facing. Turning something does not change which way it will then travel. That is why a patrol is written as "out, turn round, back" rather than as a circle.

🎯 Now change something: make your model patrol. forever holding repeat 20 times → move X by 0.3 feet, then rotate 180 degrees, then the same pair again. Out, about-turn, back, about-turn, forever.

Tutorial 6 · Coding · about 10 minutes

👥 Build an army

One block, and the thing you spent half an hour building becomes a crowd. This is the payoff for making a proper model instead of a pile of shapes.

  1. Click your robot → Program.
  2. Drag repeat 3 times across, and drop duplicate 4 ft away inside it.
  3. Save. Three copies appear in a neat line, each one four feet further along than the last.
  4. Look closely. The copies are complete, separate models — click one and you can resize it, move it or program it on its own.
  1. repeat 3 times
  2. duplicate 4 ft away

💡 Why don't the copies keep copying? Because a copy is handed everything except the duplicate block. A spinning robot makes spinning copies, but they do not breed — otherwise forever + duplicate would fill the world in about four seconds.

🎯 Now change something: before you duplicate, give the robot forever → rotate 4 degrees. Save. Then add the duplicate program. Now you have a whole row of spinning robots — and only the first one you have to think about.
