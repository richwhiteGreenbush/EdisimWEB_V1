1 🔨 Build

Four shapes. Endless models.

Open Menu → Create Model and drop a shape on the ground. A little 🔨 hammer floats over it. The hammer is the whole toolbox — click it and you can stretch the piece, turn it, paint it, and join it to its neighbours.

  1. 1

    Stretch it into shape

    Click the hammer and choose Stretch to Shape. Drag a corner and the piece grows that way — a cube becomes a wall, a plank, or a whole roof. The opposite corner stays put, so nothing drifts.

    🔄 Rotate/Move Shape adds three rings for turning. Turns snap every 15°, so square corners are actually square.

    ✥ Stretch to Shape
  2. 2

    Lift it into place

    Drag the green ball above the box to move a piece anywhere — up, down, sideways. That is how a head goes on a body and a roof goes on a house. Pieces never sink underground, so nothing gets lost.

    ✥ Move
  3. 3

    Join the pieces

    Connect to Primitive lists only the pieces that are touching this one. Tap to join them. Paint each piece with Apply Texture — a colour, or a picture that wraps right around the shape.

    🔗 Nine pieces, one robot
  4. 4

    Press Render Model

    The hammers vanish and the pile of parts becomes one solid thing. Now you can resize it, program it, save it, and copy it — just like everything else in Edusim.

    🆕 Got your own 3D models? Import a .glb file and it gets the hammer too — stretch it, join it to shapes, build with it.

    ✨ One model

2 🧩 Code

Two ways to write the same program

Click anything in any world and press Program. Start with drag-and-drop blocks. When you are ready, flip the switch to JavaScript — your blocks turn into real code you can read. Flip back any time. Nothing is ever lost.

Block Code

Snap blocks together

🧩 Program this object

Fifteen blocks in three colours: Control (repeat, forever, wait), Motion (move, glide, rotate) and Looks (say, size, colour). If you have met Scratch, you already know how this works.

forever move forward 10 feet rotate 90 degrees
JavaScript

Or type real code

Block CodeJavaScript 
// the same program, as code
await forever(async () => {
  await moveForward(10);
  await rotate(90);
});

The exact same actions, written out. Every block has a matching function, and the editor colours your code as you type. If you make a typo, Save tells you what is wrong instead of losing your work.

▶ Every programmed object gets a green play button floating above it. Programs save with the world and start themselves the next time you open it.

c. 2500 BC

Ancient Egypt

The Giza plateau, with the Sphinx in front and Khafre's pyramid rising straight behind it — the view everybody knows. Walk between the three pyramids, find the granite valley temple with not one carving on it, and the cedar ship that was buried in a pit and came out in 1,224 pieces.

  • 🦁 The Sphinx, 240ft of it
  • 🔺 All three pyramids
  • ⛵ Khufu's buried ship
  • 🎨 Carving with its paint still in it
▶ Ancient Egypt

24 August, AD 79

Ancient Pompeii

Not a ruin. Every picture you have seen of Pompeii is broken walls in sunshine, but this is the town on an ordinary working afternoon — houses standing, bar stocked, street intact — with Vesuvius going up behind it.

  • 🛞 Cart ruts and stepping stones
  • 🍲 A Roman fast-food counter
  • 🏟️ The oldest stone amphitheatre
  • 🕯️ The plaster casts, off to one side
▶ Ancient Pompeii

Rome, begun 72 AD

The Colosseum

Eighty arches round the ground floor, every one of them numbered — your pottery ticket gave you a gate, a stair and a row. The arena floor is gone, so you can see the two storeys of tunnels underneath where the lifts were.

  • 🎟️ Fifty thousand seats, numbered
  • 🕳️ The tunnels under the arena
  • 🗿 The Arch of Constantine
  • 🧱 Why two thirds of it is missing
▶ The Colosseum

Peru, about 1450

Machu Picchu

Every block was cut to fit the ones already laid, so no two are the same shape and there is no mortar anywhere — a knife blade will not go into the joints. The walls lean inward and every doorway is a trapezoid.

  • 🧩 Stone cut to fit, no mortar
  • 🌾 Terraces that drain a mountain
  • ☀️ A temple aligned to the solstice
  • 🦙 Why there are no wheels here
▶ Machu Picchu
🕌
Agra, 1632–1653

The Taj Mahal

The plan is not a square — it is a square with the corners cut off, and all eight faces carry an arch. The four minarets lean two degrees outward on purpose, so an earthquake would drop them away from the tomb.

  • 📐 Eight faces, not four
  • 🗼 Minarets that lean on purpose
  • 💎 Inlaid stone flowers
  • 🪞 A building delivered twice
▶ The Taj Mahal

Red Square

St Basil's is nine churches on one foundation, and no two of its domes are alike — spirals, chevrons, facets. None of the colour is original: it stood white with gold domes for a hundred years first.

  • 🧅 Nine domes, all different

  • 🧱 Battlements imported from Italy
  • 🕰️ A clock twenty feet across

Ellis Island

The busiest year the station ever had. Deliberately not grand: only steerage came here, to a queue, a numbered tag pinned to your coat, and a doctor who looked at your eyes for thirty seconds. The baggage is the biggest thing on the dock, and that is the point.
