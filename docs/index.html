⭐ The three big ones

Build it. Code it. Explore it.

Everything in Edusim is one of these three, and they're meant to be done in order. Make a model out of simple shapes. Teach it what to do by dragging blocks together. Then take it somewhere worth standing — into worlds that already have plenty in them waiting to be programmed.

1 🔨 Build it — the Model Builder

Four shapes is all it takes

Open Menu → Create Model and pick a shape. It lands on the ground a few steps in front of you, bright yellow, with a little 🔨 hammer floating over it. That hammer is the way in to everything else — click it and you can stretch the piece, turn it, paint it, join it to its neighbours, and finally turn the whole assembly into one solid model.

  1. 1

    Drop a shape in

    Menu → Create Model → Cube (or Sphere, or Cylinder, or Pyramid). It appears on the ground in front of you at about waist height, in build yellow, so you can always tell which pieces are still under construction and which are finished.

    ☰ Menu
    Load Object ▸ Create Model ▾ Cube Sphere Cylinder Tetrahedron Load World ▸ Clear World VR Headset View
  2. 2

    Stretch it into the shape you need

    Click the hammer, choose Stretch to Shape, and a see-through blue box appears around the piece with a handle on every corner. Drag a corner and the piece stretches — the opposite corner stays exactly where it is, so a cube becomes a plank, a post, a wall or a slab without ever drifting off the ground.

    💡 Same box, three jobs: drag a corner to stretch, drag the middle to slide the piece around, and drag the green ball floating above it to move the piece anywhere at all — up, down, left, right, forwards, back.

    🔄 And Rotate/Move Shape puts three coloured rings around it: the flat one spins the piece on the spot, the two upright ones tip it over — with the same box and ball there to move it. Turning snaps every 15°, so a roof brace at 45° really is 45°.

    ✥ Stretch to Shape
  3. 3

    Move it into place

    Pieces land on the ground, but models are rarely flat. The green ball hovering above the blue box moves the piece in every direction: drag it up and down to raise and lower, or sideways to slide it about in mid-air. That is how a head goes on a body, a roof goes on a house, and a snowman's middle ball ends up centred on its bottom one rather than hanging off the edge. It stops at ground level, so nothing can ever get buried where you can't find it again.

    ✥ Move
  4. 4

    Paint it, or wrap a picture round it

    Apply Texture gives you a row of colours to tap, a full colour picker if none of them is quite right, and an Upload an Image button. Pick a photo from your computer and it wraps around the shape — brick on a wall, bark on a tree trunk, your own face on a robot's head.

    Apply Texture Any colour Upload an Image… A picture wraps around the whole shape. Back
  5. 5

    Push pieces together and join them

    Connect to Primitive lists the pieces this one is actually touching — nothing else, so you can't accidentally weld an arm to something on the other side of the field. Tap one and they're joined. Keep going and a dozen pieces become one chain: legs to a body, a head to the body, eyes to the head.

    🔗 Twelve pieces, one robot
  6. 6

    Render it — and it becomes a real object

    Press Render Model and the hammers vanish. What was a pile of parts is now one solid model, and everything the rest of Edusim can do applies to it: click it to resize it, move it, or open the block editor and program it. It saves itself, it travels in a world file, and the duplicate block will happily make you a hundred more.

    ✨ One model

2 🧩 Code it — the block editor

Now bring it to life

The model you just built is an object like any other. Click it and choose Program, and a drag-and-drop block editor opens: the same idea as Scratch, and just as hard to get wrong. Snap the blocks into a stack, press Save, and it starts doing it right there in the world while you watch. The same thing works on anything else you can see, too — a park bench, a light orb, a Tyrannosaurus.

repeat 3 times duplicate 4.5 ft away forever rotate 5 degrees wait 0.1 seconds repeat 12 times change size by 8 %
▶ repeat 3 times → duplicate

Two blocks. One robot became four, each copy a neat step further along than the last — and the green ▶ over the original runs it again from the top.

Control

repeat · forever · wait · duplicate

Loops and timing, which is where the real thinking is. duplicate is the show-off: it copies the whole object, program and all, a set distance away.

Motion

move X / Y / Z · rotate

Slide something along a world direction, or spin it on the spot. Handy trap to know: turning an object doesn't change which way move will then send it.

Looks

change size · change colour

Grow a sapling into a tree over twelve steps, or fade a light orb between two colours all evening. Percentages compound, which surprises everybody the first time.

▶ Every programmed object gets a green play button floating above it, so you can always find what you've written and run it again. Programs save with the world and start themselves the next time you open it.

3 🗺️ Explore it — worlds to learn in

Worlds that come with something to program

You don't have to start on an empty field. Open Menu → Load World and you're standing in a park, a museum, a library, the Moon, a base on Mars, an island of full-size dinosaurs, or a walk-through tour of the human body (with more on the way). Every one is dotted with signs worth stopping for — and none of it is fixed in place. Anything you can see, you can move, resize, or teach to move on its own.

🔎 And at least one world is not on that list at all. It is in there — you just have to go and find the way in.

⚡ Activity 1 · The Park
⚡ Already built, ready to program

The challenge is standing right there in the world

Every world has two activity boards in it. Each one names a real object nearby — the geese on the pond, the mobile hanging in the museum, the Moon buggy, a Triceratops — sets it a job, and prints the exact blocks to snap together to do it.

The blocks on the sign are the same colours as the blocks in the editor, so an orange repeat 20 times on the board is the orange one you go and drag. Nothing to look up, nothing to guess at.

  • ✓ Fourteen challenges waiting across the worlds, no setup needed
  • ✓ Every sequence has been run — the boards don't promise anything the blocks can't do
  • ✓ Finish one, then change the numbers and see what breaks. That's the lesson.

The whole menu, in one picture

Two groups and that's it. Load World is where you go — every ready-made world, plus saving yours to a file and opening one back up. Create Model is the four building shapes, with Load Object tucked inside it for everything else you can bring in: your own models, pictures, lights and web panels. Opening one group closes the other, so it never turns into a wall of buttons.

🌾 And when you would rather have the horizon to yourself, My World is exactly that: open grass, a few trees for scale, and three boards at the far side setting you a rocket to build and a program to write. Nothing else in your way. It's the one to pick when the model you're building is the whole point.

🔎 One world is deliberately missing from this list. Under the Sea has no menu button at all — the only way in is a billboard standing behind the nature centre in the Park. Walk round the back of the building, click the sign, and you are on the reef.

💾 Your changes save themselves as you go, so a world is exactly how you left it when you come back. Choosing it from the menu again hands you a clean copy to start over with — and Save World writes the whole thing, models you built included, to a file you can keep or send to a friend.

🥽 View in VR

Put a headset on and stand inside it

Open Menu → VR Headset View and the picture splits in two, one for each eye. In a headset that's all it takes — you're in there. Turn your head to look around, walk with the controllers, and the menu follows you as a panel hanging in the air beside you, so you can jump to another world without taking the headset off. Standing next to something you built yourself, at its real size, is the moment this stops being a web page.

🥽

With a real headset

Open Edusim in the headset's own browser and choose VR Headset View. Your head does the looking, the thumbsticks do the walking, and you point a controller at the floating menu to pick things.

📱

With a phone

No headset needed. Slide a phone into a cardboard-style viewer and it follows the phone's own motion sensors as you look around. To choose something, just rest your eyes on it for a second.

💻

On a plain laptop

It still works. You get the two-eye view filling the screen, which is a surprisingly good way to see how VR pulls the trick off. Press Esc whenever you want to come back.

🛟 A few things still need a normal screen — the Model Builder's hammer menu, importing files, the drawing pad and placing web panels — so choosing one of those drops you out of VR first. Everything else, every world included, works without taking the headset off.

Other things to put in your world

Build, code, explore is the whole of it — but there is more to put in a world than models. A paint pad that turns drawings into 3D, real lights you can drop anywhere, a file importer, and a working web browser you can hang on a wall.

🧰 Import
Nothing is stuck

Tap anything. Change anything. Bring anything.

Click any object — a park bench, a dinosaur, somebody's liver, a robot you built this morning — and a little menu opens: bigger, smaller, nudge it somewhere better, or straight to the block editor. Import brings in your own .glb, .gltf, .obj, photos and animated GIFs, each sized to roughly the height of a person on the way in so nothing lands a mile wide or too small to find.

📱 Tablet-friendly
👉 Finger-friendly controls
Play Anywhere

A laptop, a Chromebook, or a tablet on the couch

On a computer you walk with the arrow keys and drag to look around. On a touchscreen a big D-pad appears in the corner — hold it to walk and turn, and drag anywhere else to look. For Parents & Teachers

Built for building, not for collecting data

Edusim: Web Edition runs entirely in the browser. Worlds are saved on your own device, there's no account to create, and there's nobody else in there.

🔒

No accounts, no strangers

It's a one-player sandbox. No chat, no other players, no sign-in — just your child and whatever they're making.

💾

Saves itself, right on the device

Work is kept in the browser on that device, automatically, as they go. Save World writes it out as a file to back up, hand in, or send to somebody else.

🚫

No ads, no purchases

Nothing to buy, no pop-ups, nothing to click by mistake. It's a place to build and that's all it is.

🔨

Modelling and coding in one place

Building a model out of primitives and then programming it is the same pairing a real 3D or games course teaches — composition and scale first, then sequence, loops and iteration. Here it takes about ten minutes to reach both.

🧠

Real skills, quietly picked up

Thinking in three dimensions, judging scale and colour, breaking a big shape into parts, and a genuine first go at loops and sequences with drag-and-drop blocks — the same ideas as written code, minus the syntax.

🪧

Worlds with something to say

Each ready-made world is dotted with short signs worth stopping for — park design and geology, art movements, the Dewey Decimal System, the Apollo landings, what living on Mars would actually take, life at the end of the Cretaceous, and how the organs inside you fit together. Pitched at ages 12–15, and entirely skippable if a child just wants to build.

🖥️

Runs on what you already have

It's a web page. No install, no app store, no special hardware — a school Chromebook or an old laptop is plenty. VR is optional, and everything works without it.

🌐

One thing to know about

Each world contains a working browser panel, open on the Edusim site. Children can type any address into it, so it's worth a look together the first time. The Model Builder can also load a picture from the device to wrap around a shape.

📘 There's a full set of printable training cards, step-by-step tutorials, and lesson plans for every world.

</> For Developers

The whole thing is open — go make your own version

Edusim: Web Edition is a single-page Three.js app with no backend and no build magic. Every world you just read about is generated in plain JavaScript, so there's nothing hidden: read it, change it, and make it yours.

Fork it, branch it, make it yours

Clone the repository, cut yourself a branch, and start changing things. Add a world of your own to src/props/, teach the block editor a new block, give the Model Builder a fifth shape, restyle the whole place — it's your copy, and you can't break anyone else's.

  • No backend. Pure client-side Three.js that ships as a static dist/ folder — host it anywhere.
  • Worlds are code. Each prebuilt world is a list of records and a set of prop builders, so a new one is a new file plus one entry in a table.
  • Models are records, not meshes. A model built in-app is stored as its list of parts and rebuilt from scratch every load, so it costs a few hundred bytes and survives an export.
  • Read CLAUDE.md first. It's the architecture tour, written as the project was built — including the traps that were hit along the way.
