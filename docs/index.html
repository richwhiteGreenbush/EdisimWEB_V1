✨ A 3D World You Build Yourself

Imagine, Create, Share your world!

A 3D world that runs in your browser — nothing to download, nothing to sign up for. You land in a city park. From there you can walk into an art museum, a public library, the surface of the Moon, a base on Mars, an island of full-size dinosaurs, or the inside of the human body — and change anything you find. Bring in your own models, paint a shape and watch it puff up into 3D, drop lights that really light things up, teach your creations to move with drag-and-drop code, and put on a headset to stand inside the whole thing.

🗺️ Worlds Ready to Explore

Walk into a world. Then change it.

You never start on an empty field. Open Menu → Load World and you're standing in a park, a museum, a library, the Moon, a base on Mars, an island of full-size dinosaurs, or a walk-through tour of the human body (with more on the way). Signs dotted through each one tell you what you're looking at — and none of it is fixed in place. Anything you can see, you can move somewhere better, make bigger or smaller, or teach to move on its own.

Swap worlds whenever you like

Tap ☰ Menu, then Load World, and pick one. Everything changes around you — new ground, new sky, new light — and you're set down at the front gate looking in. Anything you can add lives under Load Object instead, so the menu stays short no matter how much goes into it.

💾 Your changes save themselves as you go, so a world is exactly how you left it when you come back. Choosing it from the menu again hands you a clean copy to start over with — and Save World writes the whole thing to a file you can keep, or send to a friend to open on their own computer.

🖨️ Free printable guide

Take a training card with you

Every world has a card: three pictures of the place, the two coding activities waiting on boards inside it, and three more challenges once those are running. Print one, print the set, or read them on screen — and there is a page of lesson plans for teachers too.

🥽 View in VR

Put a headset on and stand inside it

Open Menu → VR Headset View and the picture splits in two, one for each eye. In a headset that's all it takes — you're in there. Turn your head to look around, walk with the controllers, and the menu follows you as a panel hanging in the air beside you, so you can jump to another world without taking the headset off.

🥽

With a real headset

Open Edusim in the headset's own browser and choose VR Headset View. Your head does the looking, the thumbsticks do the walking, and you point a controller at the floating menu to pick things.

📱

With a phone

No headset needed. Slide a phone into a cardboard-style viewer and it follows the phone's own motion sensors as you look around. To choose something, just rest your eyes on it for a second.

💻

On a plain laptop

It still works. You get the two-eye view filling the screen, which is a surprisingly good way to see how VR pulls the trick off. Press Esc whenever you want to come back.

🛟 A few things still need a normal screen — importing files, the drawing pad, and placing web panels — so choosing one of those drops you out of VR first. Everything else, every world included, works without taking the headset off.

☰ Menu
🧰 One tap away
Bring your own

Drop in your own models and pictures

Tap Import and drop in a 3D model, a photo, or an animated GIF. Whatever you bring gets sized to about the height of a person on the way in, so a model built at some odd scale still lands somewhere sensible instead of a mile wide or too small to find. If it comes out the wrong size anyway, one tap of Reset to 5ft tall puts it right.

  • ✓ Works with .glb, .gltf, .obj, .png, .jpg, and animated .gif files
  • ✓ Sized automatically, so nothing arrives enormous or invisible
  • ✓ Or just drag the file straight onto the window
🎨 Draw
🎈 Balloon!
Draw it into being

Paint something flat. Get something round.

Pick a color and a brush and paint whatever you like — a face, a star, a small monster. Press Done — Inflate and the drawing puffs up off the page into a rounded 3D shape, still wearing every color you put on it. No two ever come out the same, because each one is built from your drawing and nobody else's.

  • ✓ Paint with as many colors and strokes as you like
  • ✓ Curvy shapes, holes and separate blobs all inflate properly
  • ✓ Lands in your world ready to move, resize and program
💡 Light Orb
✨ Real glowing light
Light it up

Drop glowing light orbs anywhere

One tap of Light Orb drops a glowing sphere into the world. It isn't a picture of a glow — it's a real light, and you can watch it fall across the grass and up the side of whatever is standing nearby. Each new orb comes out a different color, and you can set the exact shade from the orb's own Color menu.

  • ✓ Really lights the ground, the hills and anything close by
  • ✓ A new color each time, or pick the one you want
  • ✓ The quickest way to make a dark corner worth visiting
🧩 Program this object
🧠 No coding experience needed
Coding, without the typing

Teach anything to move

Click any object and choose Program. A drag-and-drop block editor opens — the same idea as Scratch, and just as hard to get wrong. Snap blocks together to say repeat 10 times, rotate 15 degrees, wait 1 second, change color to… and the object gets on with it, right there in the world. There's a duplicate block too, so one thing can turn itself into a whole row of them.

  • ✓ Drag blocks together — nothing to type, nothing to spell wrong
  • ✓ A green ▶ floats above anything you've programmed — tap it to run it again
  • ✓ Loops, waits and repeats — the real ideas, in a form you can see working

Play Anywhere

A laptop, a Chromebook, or a tablet on the couch

On a computer you walk with the arrow keys and drag to look around. On a touchscreen a big D-pad appears in the corner — hold it to walk and turn, and drag anywhere else to look. Two fingers at once is fine, so you can walk with one thumb and look with the other.

For Parents & Teachers

Built for building, not for collecting data

Edusim: Web Edition runs entirely in the browser. Worlds are saved on your own device, there's no account to create, and there's nobody else in there.

🔒

No accounts, no strangers

It's a one-player sandbox. No chat, no other players, no sign-in — just your child and whatever they're making.

💾

Saves itself, right on the device

Work is kept in the browser on that device, automatically, as they go. Save World writes it out as a file to back up, hand in, or send to somebody else.

🚫

No ads, no purchases

Nothing to buy, no pop-ups, nothing to click by mistake. It's a place to build and that's all it is.

🧠

Real skills, quietly picked up

Thinking in three dimensions, judging scale and color, and a genuine first go at loops and sequences using drag-and-drop blocks — the same ideas as written code, minus the syntax.

🪧

Worlds with something to say

Each ready-made world is dotted with short signs worth stopping for — park design and geology, art movements, the Dewey Decimal System, the Apollo landings, what living on Mars would actually take, life at the end of the Cretaceous, and how the organs inside you fit together. Pitched at ages 12–15, and entirely skippable if a child just wants to build.

🖥️

Runs on what you already have

It's a web page. No install, no app store, no special hardware — a school Chromebook or an old laptop is plenty. VR is optional, and everything works without it.

🌐

One thing to know about

Each world contains a working browser panel, open on the Edusim site. Children can type any address into it, so it's worth a look together the first time.

</> For Developers

The whole thing is open — go make your own version

Edusim: Web Edition is a single-page Three.js app with no backend and no build magic. Every world you just read about is generated in plain JavaScript, so there's nothing hidden: read it, change it, and make it yours.

Fork it, branch it, make it yours

Clone the repository, cut yourself a branch, and start changing things. Add a world of your own to src/props/, teach the block editor a new block, restyle the whole place — it's your copy, and you can't break anyone else's.

  • No backend. Pure client-side Three.js that ships as a static dist/ folder — host it anywhere.
  • Worlds are code. Each prebuilt world is a list of records and a set of prop builders, so a new one is a new file plus one entry in a table.
  • Read CLAUDE.md first. It's the architecture tour, written as the project was built — including the traps that were hit along the way.
